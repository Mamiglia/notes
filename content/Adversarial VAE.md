Researchers have extended VAEs by adding a GAN-style discriminator on the **VAE’s outputs** to force sharper, more realistic images. A seminal example is **VAE-GAN** (Larsen et al., 2016), which treats the VAE decoder as the GAN generator. The discriminator’s learned feature-space similarity is used in place of pixel-wise loss. In Larsen et al.’s words, they “combine a variational autoencoder (VAE) with a generative adversarial network (GAN)” so that “learned feature representations in the GAN discriminator [form] the basis for the VAE reconstruction objective”[proceedings.mlr.press](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/larsen16.html#:~:text=,level%20abstract%20visual%20features%20%28e.g). This replaces crude MSE losses with a perceptual metric, yielding much sharper face images than a standard VAE. Subsequent works follow this template: they **share the decoder/generator** and append a discriminator on images, with loss terms from both VAE and GAN. For example, Khan et al. propose a VAE+GAN where an **autoencoder-based discriminator** matches real vs. fake image statistics (and even latent-space features) to guide the VAE[salman-h-khan.github.io](https://salman-h-khan.github.io/papers/WACV18.pdf#:~:text=VAE%20based%20generative%20models,model%20uses%20an%20error%20feedback). Mittal et al.’s **IDVAE** goes further by _merging_ the encoder and discriminator networks (so there is no separate discriminator)[ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.13062#:~:text=Figure%201%20illustrates%20our%20proposed,the%20encoder%20and%20the%20discriminator). In IDVAE the encoder **becomes the discriminator**, sharing all layers up to the final output. They report that this two-tier design still achieves high-fidelity samples (improving Inception/FID scores) while retaining VAE-style reconstruction, _without_ a separate adversary[ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.13062#:~:text=Figure%201%20illustrates%20our%20proposed,the%20encoder%20and%20the%20discriminator).

- **Architecture:** In VAE-GAN hybrids, the VAE’s decoder (generator) is trained with both the usual VAE loss (reconstruction + KL) and an adversarial loss from a discriminator on the image. The discriminator tries to tell apart real images from decoded (“fake”) images. (Larsen et al.’s VAE-GAN also uses the discriminator’s _internal features_ to compute a feature-matching reconstruction loss.) IDVAE shares weights between encoder and discriminator, so the model has just an encoder and decoder, with the encoder outputting both latent codes and a real/fake score[ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.13062#:~:text=Figure%201%20illustrates%20our%20proposed,the%20encoder%20and%20the%20discriminator).
- **Benefits:** These hybrids typically produce **much sharper, more realistic samples** than VAEs. For example, Larsen et al. show their model “outperforms VAEs with element-wise similarity measures in terms of visual fidelity”[proceedings.mlr.press](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/larsen16.html#:~:text=,level%20abstract%20visual%20features%20%28e.g). IDVAE reports improved FID (less mode collapse) because the adversarial signal helps cover data modes that VAE’s MSE might miss[ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.13062#:~:text=Figure%201%20illustrates%20our%20proposed,the%20encoder%20and%20the%20discriminator).
- **Challenges/Stability:** Adding a GAN component reintroduces GAN instabilities (mode collapse, training oscillation). Many authors note the need for careful balancing. Khan et al. adopt an error-feedback mechanism to keep the generator and discriminator losses in equilibrium[salman-h-khan.github.io](https://salman-h-khan.github.io/papers/WACV18.pdf#:~:text=to%20the%20VAE%20by%20matching,the%20gen%02erated%20samples%20from%20our). Mittal et al. claim that by collapsing encoder and discriminator they “sustain both reconstruction and generation” without a separate adversary, implicitly improving stability[ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.13062#:~:text=Figure%201%20illustrates%20our%20proposed,the%20encoder%20and%20the%20discriminator). In practice, such hybrids often require GAN tricks (e.g. Wasserstein or least-squares losses, gradient penalties, equilibrium heuristics) to train stably.
